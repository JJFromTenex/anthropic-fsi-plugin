# The Champion Model & Enablement Shape

## Why champions, structurally

The cautious middle (typically ~40% of any engineering org) does not convert on leadership comms or vendor demos. It converts on a trusted peer saying "I use it, here's what it's good at, here's where it's weak." Champions are the distribution network for that sentence.

## Selection

- **Volunteers first** — self-nomination signals the intrinsic motivation the role runs on; a champion roster from a survey beats one from an org chart.
- **Credibility over enthusiasm.** One respected senior/staff engineer per division outweighs five excited juniors. Recruit at least one from each *skeptical* population — a converted skeptic from the hardest-culture division is the single most persuasive asset a program can have.
- **Coverage math:** roughly 1 champion per 25–50 engineers in an active wave, mapped to divisions and locations (a champion 9 timezones away is a name on a slide, not a champion).

## The deal (both sides)

Champions get: early access, a direct line to the program team and vendor, visible recognition, and **real time** — 10–20% during their waves, actually backfilled and budgeted. Champions owe: office hours, first-response in the support channel, pattern curation (what works in *our* repos → CLAUDE.md conventions, shared commands), and honest feedback upward — a champion who only relays good news is a marketing asset, not a program asset.

The failure mode is the unfunded mandate: champions-as-a-slide with no backfill burns out exactly the trusted people the program most needs. If the budget can't fund backfill, shrink the wave, not the champion time.

## Enablement shape (what the sentiment data actually asks for)

Surveys of cautious/concerned engineers repeatedly rank the same things: a clear policy on what code can be sent; proof on *our* codebase, not demos; opt-in rather than mandate; training deeper than a 30-minute video; senior engineers they trust vouching. Design to that list:

- **In-repo beats classroom.** Seeded CLAUDE.md, curated slash commands, worked examples on the org's own legacy code. The good path becomes the default path.
- **Differentiate by cohort.** A staff engineer on a payments ledger, a junior on a React app, and a quant writing models need different first sessions. One-size training reads as "another tool mandate."
- **Adjacent roles get their own track.** BAs (SQL, analysis), QA (test authoring), quants (models, notebooks), PMs (PRDs, specs), SRE (runbooks, toil automation): a 2-hour use-case-led path, not a compressed engineering course.
- **Publish wins wave-over-wave.** Each wave's measured results and best patterns are the onboarding material for the next — the program compounds.
- **Reserve white-glove for where it pays:** regulated-tier cohorts (where attestation workflows must be taught anyway) and strategic skeptic groups. Everyone else: champion-led + self-serve + in-repo.
