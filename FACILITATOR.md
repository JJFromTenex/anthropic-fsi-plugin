# Project Catalyst — Facilitator Guide

## Preparation target: 30 minutes

1. Open the hosted portal and create a workshop.
2. Open the shared market display in the room.
3. Confirm the team driver’s Claude Code connection.
4. Review the baseline decision, sector alert, and final board question.
5. Run one accelerated rehearsal through the instructor controls.

## Standard 90-minute run

| Time | Stage |
|---:|---|
| 0–10 | Teams enter, name their banks, and claim their first contributions |
| 10–18 | Deliver the mandate and explain the decision canvas |
| 18–30 | Attendees investigate resources and make the baseline decision |
| 30–40 | Stakeholder review and one revision |
| 40–45 | Market update and short debrief |
| 45–50 | Release the sector-wide regulatory alert |
| 50–65 | Teams investigate new evidence and revise their strategy |
| 65–75 | Final stakeholder and board review |
| 75–82 | Close the market and compare approaches |
| 82–90 | Participants record the owner, missing evidence, and next action for their organization |

## Driver posture

The driver manages time and participation. The driver does not research, prompt Claude, vote, or make the decision. At each stage, read the decision aloud, invite attendees to claim the available work individually or in pairs, then reconvene around one shared recommendation. If one attendee dominates, ask which contribution or source has not yet been heard.

## Hints and clues

Release support privately and progressively. Using support never affects market position.

1. **Hint:** ask a coaching question.
2. **Clue:** point to the relevant evidence category.
3. **Source clue:** identify a useful resource.

Coach the information need, not the answer. Useful interventions include:

- “What evidence would let you choose between those options?”
- “Which assumption has not been tested?”
- “Who owns this decision, and what tradeoff are they accepting?”
- “What can continue safely even if one population must wait?”

## Tone

The Catalyst Exchange explains consequences; it is not an arcade game. Avoid public failure language. Use “board-ready,” “revision requested,” “evidence needed,” and “decision pending.” Recognize different strengths rather than overemphasizing last place.
